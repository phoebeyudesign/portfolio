Snyk Learn Assignments:
learning as an operational tool.

Snyk Learn enables AppSec teams to assign secure coding lessons and track learner progress at scale. This project improved how assignments are created and monitored across large groups of engineers.

LEARN

Learn overview

Turning learning into an operational security product.

I led end-to-end product design for the Assignments experience, partnering closely with product and engineering to define scope, explore solutions, and deliver a focused early release.

The impact

From passive content library to a mission-critical compliance tool.

Assignments became the foundation of Snyk Learn’s first enterprise tier, with beta organisations converting to paid through to the full V1 launch.

01

The problem

Strong content, no operational control.

Snyk Learn offered high-quality, bite-sized lessons, but AppSec teams still had to share content through LMS platforms, email, or Slack.

These workarounds didn’t scale and made it difficult to:

01

Distribute

Relevant lessons to the right teams.

02

Track

Completion and learner progress.

03

Generate

Evidence for audits and compliance.

Core tension

AppSec faced high effort and low visibility, with no reliable way to prove learning impact. This gap led us to design the Assignments experience.

02

Jobs-to-be-Done

From pattern to Jobs-to-be-Done.

To understand how AppSec managed learning assignments, and where friction occurred, I translated the problem into core Jobs-to-be-Done and mapped the end-to-end assignment journey.

  • Surface unmet needs and workflow inefficiencies
  • Align the team around the user’s real job
  • Prioritise where Assignments could deliver the most impact
03

OOUX exploration

Defining the system.

Snyk Learn already included lessons, learning paths and certificates. A new module needed to integrate cleanly into that ecosystem.

I mapped the object relationships to reduce ambiguity across design and engineering, and to identify the core content and metadata an assignment actually contains.

04

Scope & task flow

Choosing what matters.

Mapping the full journey revealed that Assignments was not one interaction, but a system spanning six tasks: create, edit, delete, view progress, send reminders and download reports.

Because this was a new enterprise workflow, we avoided building the entire lifecycle upfront and aligned on the minimum set of tasks needed to validate value.

01

Included in early version

Create assignments, view assignment progress, download reports.

02

Intentionally deferred

Edit assignments, delete assignments, send reminders.

Decision

This reduced system complexity, accelerated delivery, and let us validate the core enterprise use case before expanding.

05

Designing the experience

From flow to interface.

Early exploration mapped the assignment journey and found where clarity and efficiency could be improved. These flows validated the release scope and shaped how key actions were structured.

Engineering flagged that assignment titles would need a new data model and learning paths would add significant complexity. We cut both from the first release, prioritising speed and validated learning over completeness.

Early model

Create assignments by selecting lessons and due dates, then view progress and completion status. Nothing more until real usage proved the need.

Key UI screens

AppSec can select an assignment, quickly create new ones, review progress in a structured table, and export data as a CSV for analysis and audit reporting.

06

Outcome & impact

From passive library to compliance tool.

Assignments let AppSec teams distribute training at scale, monitor completion in one view, and export audit-ready reports.

By turning a passive library into a compliance workflow, the work unlocked the product’s first enterprise monetisation initiative. After launch, paid adoption and revenue grew significantly within six months, and we moved straight into edit, delete and reminders on the path to V1.
