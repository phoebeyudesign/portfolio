Rethinking Snyk Analytics:
from data to decisions.

Snyk Analytics surfaced large volumes of data, but made it difficult for stakeholders to understand overall security posture or answer critical questions without navigating across multiple reports.

ANALYTICS

Analytics overview

Redefining Analytics not as a collection of charts, but as a system that enables decision making across security and engineering teams.

I partnered with PMs, engineers, and fellow designers, facilitating stakeholder workshops to validate the idea and align on direction.

I led discovery, facilitated stakeholder alignment, defined the analytics model, and proposed the product direction in collaboration with PMs, engineers, and design system partners.

The problem

Data-rich, but insight-poor.

The existing Analytics experience surfaced data, but did not surface the metrics needed to support decisions.

01

No high-level view

No high-level view of security performance across the organisation.

02

Answers took effort

Users had to proactively navigate reports and filters to piece together answers.

Data, not decisions

Key metrics for decisions were missing from the product.

“Are we improving?” “Where are the biggest risks?”
02

Discovery

What do AppSec teams need to understand their security posture?

The discovery opportunity: what’s required for AppSec teams to understand the overall security posture, or to make prioritization decisions.

Research methods

Workshop sessions with customers.

  • LeftTop-of-mind metrics exercise Uncover which numbers users instinctively track or report first.
  • RightWireframe design review Tested early explorations with internal stakeholders and select customers.
03

Jobs-to-be-Done

From patterns to a new analytics model.

Based on the key insights, I identified five core Jobs-to-be-Done representing the primary decision contexts across two personas.

Strategic · Leadership

A holistic, high-level view for decision making.

  • Job 01Strategic evaluation Are we improving overall?
  • Job 02Investment justification Is this worth continued investment?

Operational · AppSec teams

A detailed investigative view to manage and reduce risk.

  • Job 03Investigation & prioritization Where should we focus?
  • Job 04Monitoring & reporting What’s our current status?
  • Job 05Collaboration How do we align teams and communicate progress?
04

Analytics model

Analytics is not linear exploration. It’s a decision loop.

Zooming out from the JTBD, a universal analytics flow emerged.

01

Question

Each begins with an intent or a prompt.

  • Strategic persona: “Are we improving overall?”
  • Operational persona: “Where are the risks?”
02

Explore data

They navigate metrics or drill deeper into details.

03

Summarize & interpret

They review trends, patterns, or comparisons to form an understanding of the situation.

04

Act on insights

They take action based on findings.

  • Strategic persona: Shares or presents summaries to leadership.
  • Operational persona: Prioritizes issues, assigns tasks, or communicates progress.
The backbone

This model became the backbone for the new Analytics homepage, enabling leadership to understand overall security posture while allowing AppSec teams to drill into risk and prioritize what to fix.

05

Initial ideas

Dashboard + AI prompts.

The research pointed to two distinct user needs happening at different moments: a trusted shared baseline for “where are we overall?”, and a faster path to specific answers when investigating.

A single metrics dashboard addressed the first but left users navigating filters for the second. Adding AI prompts solved this. Users could ask “which teams have the lowest remediation rate?” directly, rather than drilling through reports to find the same answer.

Design decision

Question-first rather than navigation-first, directly addressing the core inefficiency the research surfaced.

Interaction flow

The early interaction model mapped the user journey.

User journey

The shift toward a question-first analytics experience.

  • ExploreExplore data → drill into relevant chart or view
  • AskAsk a question → result → refine → drill further
  • ActSelect a view → interpret → act
06

Visualization principles

Four principles for a data visualization library that scales.

To support the long-term growth of Snyk’s data visualization library, I defined four key principles to guide how analytics visuals should be designed as the system scales.

01

Highlight what matters

Reduce noise and surface trends that drive decisions, not just data volume.

02

Progressive disclosure

Structure information in layers: overview → detail → raw data, so users aren’t overwhelmed upfront.

03

Contextual clarity

Always pair data with the units and comparisons needed to interpret it (%, count, etc.).

04

Consistency

Standardize chart types and interaction patterns so users build familiarity across different reports.

07 / Delivery & impact

A new model for next-generation Analytics.

This work aligned product, engineering, and leadership around a shared approach to how insights are discovered and acted on.

It established a decision-first foundation built on four pieces.

  • 01A question-first analytics flow
  • 02A centralized metrics layer for shared understanding
  • 03Layered drill-through to support deeper investigation while reducing cognitive load
  • 04Early exploration of AI-assisted querying for ad-hoc analysis
What happened next

While not all concepts shipped immediately, the work directly influenced product direction, particularly the emphasis on top-level security metrics and decision-first workflows.
