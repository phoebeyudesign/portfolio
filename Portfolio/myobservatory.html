Redesigning MyObservatory:
weather you can act on.

MyObservatory is one of Hong Kong’s most widely used public weather platforms, serving millions of residents who rely on it for real-time, location-specific decisions.

MYO

MyObservatory overview

Transforming a public weather platform into an actionable, citizen-focused experience.

I led research, problem definition, interaction design, and iterative prototyping.

I partnered with product, engineering, and meteorology teams to reframe the homepage around real user behaviours and reduce friction in accessing time-sensitive information.

The outcome

A high-visibility dashboard that surfaces what matters.

Urgent warnings and essential weather data at a glance, plus a new design system that reduced development overhead and sped up new features.

01

The brief

From a narrow ask to a strategic opportunity.

“Redesign the homepage to give users faster access to rainfall products.”

I expanded this narrowly scoped ask into a more strategic opportunity: improving how citizens interpret weather information and make decisions in high-stakes moments.

02

The problem

Critical information, buried.

Many residents rely on MyObservatory for real-time weather alerts, but critical information was buried behind menus or masked by low-contrast UI.

Rainfall warnings, lightning alerts and favourite locations were hard to find, causing missed warnings and frustration, especially during urgent weather events.

03

Research

Three behavioural patterns.

Through interviews, usage analysis and contextual inquiry, I uncovered three patterns that revealed why users were struggling.

01

Buried essentials

The 9-Day Forecast, lightning alerts and favourite locations were hidden or inconsistent, reducing trust in urgent conditions.

02

Feature overload

Many tools were designed for expert meteorologists, not everyday citizens, creating noise and slowing comprehension.

03

Low legibility

Photo backgrounds and light text weakened contrast, especially outdoors, making critical updates easy to miss.

Design decision

These patterns steered the team away from complex forecasting widgets that wouldn’t solve the real problem: helping people understand what action to take next.

04

Jobs-to-be-Done

From insights to Jobs-to-be-Done.

The behaviours were translated into JTBD that aligned product, engineering and meteorology teams.

Everyday

Journey and daily planning.

  • WhenI’m planning my day or deciding whether to travel I want to quickly understand what the weather means for my plans.
  • SoI can make informed decisions Go, delay, cancel or prepare, without digging through complex data.

Urgent

Responding to severe weather.

  • WhenA weather warning is issued I want clear, trustworthy and easy-to-verify information.
  • SoI can respond confidently And take the right actions to stay safe.
05

Design principles

Simple principles to keep everyone aligned.

Based on stakeholder requirements and user interview insights, I created a set of simple design principles to guide decisions and keep the team and stakeholders aligned.

06

Wireframes

Visualising solutions with stakeholders.

I created wireframes to visualise solutions and collect stakeholder feedback.

Stakeholder feedback

  • Expected a few additional forecasting widgets.
  • Requested the chatbot remain always visible.
  • Preferred real-time location photos instead of illustrations.
07

Prototyping & testing

Testing with users and developers.

I created a clickable prototype and ran iterative usability tests and design review sessions with developers.

01

User feedback

Some weather terms were too technical. Many didn’t realise they could swipe horizontally. Users wanted quicker access to warnings and rainfall.

02

Dev team feedback

Concern about mixing warnings with other shortcuts, and that too many images and data would affect app performance.

08

Final design

A cleaner, more legible dashboard.

I also documented foundational patterns, including typography scale, contrast rules, iconography, component behaviours and responsive layout, giving the team a reusable structure it previously lacked.

  • A cleaner, more legible dashboard for quick scanning
  • Consistent warning hierarchy to support public safety
  • More accessible rainfall and environmental data
  • Simplified switching between locations
